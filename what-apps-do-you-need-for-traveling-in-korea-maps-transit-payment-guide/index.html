Your phone’s home screen might be the single most useful thing you pack for Korea.
Between mapping a subway transfer, splitting a taxi fare, and reading a restaurant menu, the right apps do more work than any guidebook.
Here’s exactly which ones to download before you land, sorted by what you’ll actually use them for.

What Apps Do You Actually Need for Traveling in Korea?

You need six categories of apps for a smooth trip to Korea: a map app for navigation, a transit app or card for subways and buses, a translation app for reading menus and signs, a payment app for tapping in without a Korean bank account, a reservation app for popular restaurants, and a government safety app for emergency alerts. You don’t need dozens of apps — most travelers get by comfortably with five or six installed before departure. Google Maps barely works for walking or driving directions inside Korea, so a local map app isn’t optional the way it might be elsewhere. Once you’ve sorted out your visa or K-ETA for entry and picked a SIM card, eSIM, or WiFi egg to get connected, apps are really the next layer of trip prep — the tools that make each day easier once you’re actually on the ground. See our Korea entry guide if you haven’t confirmed your visa or K-ETA status yet.

  • Navigation: Naver Map
  • Transit: Kakao Metro (or Naver Map’s built-in transit routes) + Mobile T-money + Let’s Korail
  • Translation: Papago
  • Payment: WOWPASS
  • Restaurant reservations: Catchtable
  • Emergency & safety: Emergency Ready app

📌 Where this list comes from:
This guide is built around the official app recommendations published by the Korea Tourism Organization on VisitKorea’s “Helpful Apps & Resources” page, cross-checked against what’s actually useful day to day.

Which Apps Help You Get Around Korea? (Maps & Transit)

For getting around Korea, Naver Map is your primary navigation app and Mobile T-money is your transit payment method, with Let’s Korail added if you’re taking KTX or intercity trains. Google Maps is missing walking, driving, and full transit directions inside Korea due to national mapping data restrictions, so almost every local and long-term resident defaults to Naver Map instead. It covers walking, cycling, driving, and public transit routes, plus real-time bus and subway arrival times, business hours, and reviews. For actually paying your fare, Mobile T-money turns your phone into a tap-to-pay transit card that works on subways, buses, and even in many taxis, and it now accepts international credit cards for top-ups — no trip to a convenience store required. If you’re heading beyond the city on KTX, SRT, or a regional express train, Let’s Korail handles ticket booking directly from your phone.

Naver Map

  • Korea’s most widely used map app, built specifically for local streets, addresses, and transit data
  • Walking, cycling, driving, and public transit route options in one search
  • Business hours, reviews, and bookmarking so you can save spots before your trip
  • Available in English — switch the display language in the app’s settings menu

Getting Around by Subway and Bus

Naver Map’s built-in transit routing already covers most subway and bus trips, showing you exact platforms, transfer walking distances, and next-departure times. Kakao Metro is a dedicated subway-only app some travelers prefer for its cleaner line diagrams and transfer visuals, particularly useful in transfer-heavy stations. Either works — pick whichever interface feels more intuitive to you, or just rely on Naver Map’s transit tab and skip installing a second app.

Mobile T-money

  • Turns your smartphone into a tap-to-pay transit card for subways, buses, and many taxis
  • Accepts international credit cards for top-ups, so you don’t need a Korean bank account or cash
  • Works alongside a physical T-money card if you’d rather keep a plastic backup

Let’s Korail

  • Official app for booking KTX, SRT, and other intercity/tourist train tickets
  • Supports the Korail Pass, a discounted multi-day rail pass built for international travelers
  • Lets you pull up your e-ticket on your phone instead of printing anything

Which Translation App Should You Use in Korea?

Papago is the translation app to use in Korea — it’s built by Naver specifically for Korean and consistently outperforms general-purpose translation apps on Korean grammar, menus, and signage. It handles typed text, photos of menus or signs, handwriting, and voice input across 14 languages, and its camera translation mode is genuinely useful for restaurant menus that only exist in Korean. Download it before your trip and test the camera-translate feature once so you’re not fumbling with it mid-restaurant on day one. It also works reasonably well offline for common phrases if you download the offline language pack in advance, which matters if you’re relying on a prepaid SIM with limited data.

✅ Get the camera translation ready before you need it
Open Papago once after downloading and grant camera permissions, then do a test scan of any Korean text nearby. Doing this at your hotel is much easier than fumbling through a permissions prompt at a restaurant counter with a line behind you.

Which Payment App Should You Use in Korea?

WOWPASS is the payment app most international travelers use in Korea — it’s an all-in-one prepaid card built specifically for visitors, combining a transit card and a debit-style card you can use almost anywhere that accepts local payment. You load money onto it using foreign cash or a foreign card at WOWPASS kiosks found in subway stations and tourist areas, and it can be topped up again at convenience stores once you’re set up. Because a large share of small vendors, markets, and even some taxis in Korea still don’t accept foreign-issued credit cards smoothly, having a Korean-linked prepaid card removes a surprising amount of daily friction. It’s not the only option — some travelers get by on a no-foreign-transaction-fee credit card alone — but WOWPASS is the closest thing to a universal local payment method built for tourists. See our currency exchange and payment methods guide for how it compares to cash, cards, and ATM withdrawals.

📌 Where to load a prepaid travel card:
Kiosks are concentrated in Seoul subway stations, Incheon Airport, and major tourist districts. Bring either foreign cash or a foreign-issued card — both work at the kiosk.

How Do You Book a Restaurant Table in Korea?

Catchtable is the app to book a restaurant table in Korea — it covers everything from high-end dining rooms to popular casual spots, and lets you see real-time wait times and read customer reviews before you commit. Many of Seoul’s most in-demand restaurants only take reservations through Catchtable, or fill up so quickly that walking in without one means a long wait or no seat at all, especially on weekends. The app shows available time slots directly, so you can book ahead instead of guessing whether a place will have room when you arrive. If you’re more interested in delivery than dine-in — say you’re staying somewhere without easy restaurant access — Shuttle Delivery covers food, and even some clothing and cosmetics delivery, in Seoul, Pyeongtaek, Daegu, and Busan.

✅ Book ahead for popular restaurants
If there’s a specific restaurant on your list, check Catchtable a few days before you plan to go rather than the same morning — the most talked-about spots can book out days in advance.

Which Apps Keep You Safe in an Emergency in Korea?

The Emergency Ready app is the official app to have for safety in Korea — it’s built by the Korean government, available in 21 languages, and covers disaster alerts, nearby shelters, emergency medical centers, police stations, and fire stations, along with basic guidance like CPR steps, first aid, and how to find a fire extinguisher nearby. Separately, Korea also sends emergency disaster text alerts (heat waves, air quality warnings, earthquakes, and similar) automatically to phones connected to a Korean mobile network — this isn’t a downloadable app, it arrives through your carrier the same way an Amber Alert would elsewhere, so if you’re using a local SIM or eSIM you’ll typically start receiving these without doing anything. For everything else, the 1330 Korea Travel Hotline connects you to a real person in multiple languages.

⚠️ Charge a power bank before you rely on any of these apps.
None of these apps help if your phone dies. If you’re carrying a spare battery or power bank for a long day out, check the airline rules first — see our spare batteries and power banks guide for the 100Wh/160Wh limits before you fly.

  • Police: 112
  • Fire & ambulance: 119
  • Korea Travel Hotline (English, Japanese, Chinese, Russian, Vietnamese, Thai, Malay, Indonesian): +82-2-1330

FAQ: Korea Travel Apps

What apps do you need for traveling in Korea?

You need a map app (Naver Map), a transit payment method (Mobile T-money), a translation app (Papago), a payment app (WOWPASS), a restaurant reservation app (Catchtable), and a government safety app (Emergency Ready app). Most travelers get by with just these five or six installed before departure.

Does Google Maps work in Korea?

Not well. Due to national mapping data restrictions, Google Maps can’t provide walking, driving, or full public transit directions inside Korea. Naver Map is the local alternative almost everyone — residents and tourists alike — uses instead.

Do I need a physical T-money card, or is the app enough?

Mobile T-money on your phone works the same way a physical card does for subways, buses, and many taxis, and it accepts international card top-ups. A physical card is a reasonable backup if your phone battery is a concern, but it isn’t required if you’re comfortable relying on the app.

What’s the best translation app for Korean?

Papago, built by Naver specifically for Korean, generally outperforms general-purpose translation apps for Korean text, menus, and signage. It supports typed text, photo/camera translation, handwriting, and voice input across 14 languages.

Can I pay for things in Korea without a Korean bank account?

Yes. WOWPASS is a prepaid card built for this exact situation — you load it with foreign cash or a foreign card at kiosks in subway stations and tourist areas, then use it for transit and everyday purchases without needing a local bank account.

Do I need to book restaurants in advance in Korea?

Not always, but for popular or well-known restaurants in Seoul and other major cities, yes — many only take reservations through Catchtable, or fill up fast enough that walking in means a long wait. Check Catchtable a few days ahead for anywhere specific you want to try.

How do emergency disaster alerts work for tourists in Korea?

If you’re using a Korean mobile network (a local SIM or eSIM), you’ll automatically receive emergency disaster text alerts the same way locals do — no app required. The Emergency Ready app adds more detail on top of that: shelters, emergency facilities nearby, and basic safety guidance, available in 21 languages.

Should I download these apps before I arrive in Korea, or can I wait?

Download and set up navigation, translation, and payment apps before you arrive if possible — WOWPASS and Mobile T-money in particular are much easier to configure with reliable Wi-Fi at home than while jet-lagged and trying to find airport Wi-Fi.

Still have questions? Contact the Foreigners’ General Information Center at 1345 (Weekdays 09:00–22:00), or the Korea Travel Hotline at 1330.
