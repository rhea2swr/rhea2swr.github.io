Thinking about bringing your dog or cat with you to Korea?
It’s absolutely possible — but Korea treats it as a formal animal quarantine process, not a simple carry-on item.
Here’s exactly what to prepare before you fly and what happens when your pet lands.

Can You Bring Your Pet to Korea?

Yes, dogs and cats can enter Korea, but only if you complete a formal quarantine process before you fly and follow inspection steps the moment you land. Bringing a pet into Korea isn’t just a travel logistics question like choosing a carrier size — it’s treated as an animal import matter, which means your pet needs a government-certified quarantine document from your home country before departure. That document must show two things: a microchip identification number and the results of a rabies neutralizing antibody test. The certificate has to be issued or endorsed by your home country’s official animal quarantine authority (EU travelers can use an EU Pet Passport instead of a separate certificate). If any of this paperwork is missing or incomplete when you arrive, Korean quarantine officers can hold your pet in an isolation facility instead of letting it go through with you. Because of this, pet owners should start preparing weeks in advance, not the week before the flight.

📌 At a glance — what your pet needs:
A microchip with its ID number recorded on your quarantine certificate, a rabies neutralizing antibody test (FAVN or FRRIT) result on that same certificate, advance approval from your airline, and — on arrival — a separate hand-off of that certificate to a quarantine officer before you clear customs.

What Documents Does Your Pet Need Before Flying to Korea?

Before your pet can legally fly to Korea, two specific medical steps have to be documented on an official quarantine certificate issued by your home country’s government. First, your pet needs a microchip implant, and that microchip’s identification number must be written into the certificate — it’s how Korean quarantine officers match the paperwork to the actual animal at arrival. Second, your pet needs a rabies neutralizing antibody test, using either the FAVN (Fluorescent Antibody Virus Neutralization) or FRRIT method, and those results also have to appear on the certificate. Neither requirement is optional, and neither can be added after the fact at the airport — both need to be completed and documented before you leave your home country. If you’re an EU traveler, an EU Pet Passport that already contains this information can generally be used in place of a separately issued certificate, but double-check with Korea’s Animal and Plant Quarantine Agency (APQA) before you travel.

The Two Non-Negotiable Requirements

  • Microchip implant with the ID number listed on the quarantine certificate
  • Rabies neutralizing antibody test (FAVN or FRRIT) with results listed on the same certificate

📌 Confirm exact requirements for your country and animal:
Quarantine paperwork requirements can vary slightly depending on your departure country and species. Check the official Animal and Plant Quarantine Agency (APQA) pet import page well before booking your flight.

What Else Should You Arrange Before You Fly?

Paperwork for your pet isn’t the only thing to line up before departure — you also need to confirm your pet actually has a seat on the plane and a place to stay once you land. Airlines set their own limits on how many pets are allowed per flight, so requesting pet travel at check-in with no advance notice is risky; you could be turned away even with valid quarantine documents in hand. Contact your airline as early as possible to get pet travel pre-approved, ideally when you book your own ticket. Separately, not every hotel, guesthouse, or attraction in Korea welcomes pets, so it’s worth confirming pet policies for your accommodation and any planned stops before you commit to an itinerary built around traveling together. Sorting out both of these logistics early means the only thing left to manage close to your trip is the quarantine paperwork itself, rather than scrambling on multiple fronts at once.

✅ Book pet travel when you book your own ticket.
Pet seats per flight are limited and policies vary by airline, so requesting approval at the same time you book your own seat avoids being turned away at check-in.

What Happens When You Arrive in Korea With a Pet?

Once you land in Korea, your pet’s paperwork gets handled separately from your own entry process. While still on the plane, you’ll fill out a standard customs declaration form, the same one every arriving traveler completes. But your pet’s quarantine certificate isn’t part of that customs paperwork — it has to be handed directly to an animal quarantine officer before you go through the customs inspection line, not folded into your general customs declaration. Inside the airport terminal itself, your pet is expected to stay inside its carrier at all times; airport staff generally only allow a short leash (no more than about 50 cm) or a pet stroller in unavoidable situations, such as brief comfort breaks. If you’re also carrying food items along with your pet, keep in mind that those go through a separate declaration and inspection track — our guide on customs rules for meat, fruit, and agricultural products covers that process in detail.

  • Customs declaration form — filled out during the flight, same as every traveler
  • Quarantine certificate — submitted separately to an animal quarantine officer before customs inspection
  • Inside the terminal — pet must stay in its carrier, except for brief unavoidable moments (short leash under 50 cm, or pet stroller)

For a full walkthrough of the general customs declaration form and how it works alongside items like duty-free allowances, see our Immigration and Customs guide.

What If Your Pet Doesn’t Meet Korea’s Quarantine Requirements?

If your pet’s paperwork doesn’t fully meet Korea’s quarantine requirements, it won’t be allowed to leave the airport with you right away. Instead, animal quarantine officers will place your pet in a separate isolation facility until the missing requirement — whether that’s an incomplete microchip record, a missing or outdated rabies antibody test, or an uncertified document — can be resolved. This isn’t a quick counter-side fix; depending on what’s missing, resolving it can take significant time and may involve additional testing, veterinary coordination, and facility fees you’d be responsible for — as is standard practice for quarantine holds, though exact costs and timelines vary by case, so confirm specifics with APQA if this situation applies to you. This is exactly why the quarantine certificate, microchip, and rabies antibody test all need to be finalized before you ever board your flight, rather than treated as something you can sort out on arrival. Travelers who complete every requirement in advance rarely encounter this step at all — it exists as a safety net for incomplete cases, not a routine part of entry.

⚠️ Incomplete paperwork means isolation, not a warning.
There’s no on-the-spot exception for missing microchip data or rabies test results — resolving an incomplete quarantine certificate after arrival takes time and money, so confirm every requirement before departure.

FAQ: Pet Quarantine and Entry Questions Answered

Can you bring your pet to Korea?

Yes. Dogs and cats can enter Korea if you complete a government-certified quarantine process before departure — a microchip implant, a rabies neutralizing antibody test (FAVN or FRRIT), and a quarantine certificate listing both — and follow the arrival procedure, which includes handing that certificate to a quarantine officer before customs.

What is a FAVN or FRRIT test, and why does my pet need one?

FAVN (Fluorescent Antibody Virus Neutralization) and FRRIT are rabies neutralizing antibody blood tests that confirm your pet has an adequate immune response to rabies vaccination. Korea requires the results of one of these tests to be listed on your pet’s quarantine certificate before entry.

Do I need a separate quarantine certificate if I’m from an EU country?

Generally, an EU Pet Passport that already includes your pet’s microchip ID and rabies antibody test results can be used in place of a separately issued quarantine certificate. Confirm the current rule with Korea’s Animal and Plant Quarantine Agency before you travel.

Can I get my pet’s microchip and rabies test done right before my flight?

No — both the microchip implant and the rabies neutralizing antibody test results need to be completed and documented on your quarantine certificate before you leave your home country. Neither can be arranged or added after you’ve already booked a last-minute trip, so start the process well in advance.

What happens at the airport if my pet’s documents aren’t in order?

Your pet will be placed in a separate isolation facility until the missing requirement is resolved, rather than being allowed to leave the airport with you. This can take time and, as is standard practice for quarantine holds, may involve facility fees — so it’s far simpler to confirm every document before departure.

Is my pet’s quarantine certificate the same as my customs declaration form?

No. The customs declaration form is filled out during the flight like any other traveler’s paperwork, while the quarantine certificate is a separate document handed directly to an animal quarantine officer before you go through customs.

Does bringing pet food count the same as bringing my pet?

No — they’re handled under different rules. Your pet goes through the animal quarantine process described here, while pet food and other food items you’re carrying fall under separate customs and agricultural import rules. See our guide to bringing food into Korea for those details.

Do I still need a visa or K-ETA if I’m traveling with a pet?

Yes — your pet’s quarantine paperwork is completely separate from your own entry requirements. You’ll still need a visa, K-ETA, or applicable waiver based on your nationality. See our Visa & K-ETA guide for details.

Still have questions about traveling with your pet? Contact the Foreigners’ General Information Center at 1345 (Weekdays 09:00–22:00), or check with Korea’s Animal and Plant Quarantine Agency directly.
