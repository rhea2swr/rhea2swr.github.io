Trying to figure out how much money you actually need for a trip to Korea?
The honest answer is: it depends heavily on your travel style — and the gap between a backpacker budget and a comfortable one is bigger than most first-time visitors expect.
This guide breaks down real daily costs for accommodation, food, transport, and attractions, plus practical ways to spend less without feeling like you’re missing out.

How Much Does a Trip to Korea Cost Per Day?

A trip to Korea can cost anywhere from around 50,000–70,000 KRW (roughly $35–50) a day if you travel like a backpacker, up to 250,000 KRW (roughly $185) or more a day for a comfortable, hotel-and-taxi kind of trip. The biggest swing factor is accommodation, followed by how often you eat at restaurants versus grabbing food from convenience stores or markets. For a first-time visitor sticking mostly to Seoul, a realistic mid-range budget — a clean private guesthouse or 3-star hotel, a mix of casual and sit-down meals, and public transport with the occasional taxi — lands around 120,000–180,000 KRW (about $90–135) per person, per day, not counting flights. Below, you’ll find typical costs broken down by accommodation, food, transportation, and attractions, plus a sample daily budget for three travel styles so you can estimate a full trip total before you book anything.

📌 These are general estimates, not official figures.
Prices vary by season, city, and exchange rate — treat the numbers below as a planning starting point, and check current prices closer to your travel dates.

How Much Should You Budget for Accommodation in Korea?

Accommodation is usually the single biggest line item in a Korea travel budget, and it’s also where your daily cost varies the most. A dorm bed in a well-reviewed hostel or guesthouse typically runs about 25,000–35,000 KRW (roughly $18–26) a night in Seoul or Busan, while a private room in a budget guesthouse or motel is closer to 45,000–70,000 KRW (about $33–52). Mid-range 3-star hotels generally fall between 80,000–130,000 KRW (around $59–96) a night, and 4–5 star hotels routinely start at 200,000 KRW (about $148) and climb well past that in central Seoul during peak season. Booking weekday stays instead of weekends, and choosing Korea Quality-certified accommodations for a reliable minimum standard, are both easy ways to keep this line item predictable.

  • Hostel dorm bed: 25,000–35,000 KRW/night ($18–26)
  • Budget guesthouse or motel, private room: 45,000–70,000 KRW/night ($33–52)
  • 3-star hotel: 80,000–130,000 KRW/night ($59–96)
  • 4–5 star hotel: 200,000 KRW/night and up ($148+)

How Much Does Food Cost in Korea Each Day?

You can eat very well in Korea on a modest budget, largely because convenience stores, street food stalls, and casual local restaurants are both cheap and genuinely good. A convenience store meal (triangle kimbap, instant noodles, a drink) runs about 5,000–8,000 KRW (roughly $4–6), a bowl of noodles or a street food snack at a market is typically 4,000–10,000 KRW (about $3–7), and a casual sit-down meal at a local restaurant — often with several free side dishes (banchan) included — lands around 8,000–15,000 KRW (roughly $6–11) per person. A traveler eating this way can cover food for about 20,000–30,000 KRW (around $15–22) a day. Choosing nicer restaurants, cafes, or Korean BBQ for dinner instead pushes a daily food budget closer to 40,000–60,000 KRW (about $30–44) or more.

How Much Should You Budget for Getting Around Korea?

Public transportation in Korea is fast, clean, and inexpensive compared to many other countries, which makes it easy to keep this part of your budget low. A single subway or bus ride in Seoul generally costs around 1,400–1,600 KRW (about $1–1.20), with a small extra fee for longer distances, and transferring between subway and bus within a set window is free or heavily discounted when you tap a rechargeable transit card like T-money. A traveler relying mainly on subway and bus can expect to spend roughly 5,000–10,000 KRW (about $4–7) a day on local transport. Taxis are metered and reasonably priced for short trips but add up quickly if used often, and travelers covering multiple cities should look into a KORAIL Pass, which offers unlimited train travel for a set number of days and can be more economical than buying individual long-distance tickets.

How Much Do Attractions and Activities Cost in Korea?

Sightseeing in Korea can be almost entirely free if you plan around it, which makes this one of the easiest categories to trim from a tight budget. Major national museums, including the National Museum of Korea and the Gyeongju National Museum, offer free admission, and free experience spaces like HiKR Ground (run by the Korea Tourism Organization) let you try Korean culture activities at no cost. Natural attractions — Han River parks, hiking trails, and most beaches — are also free to enter. Paid attractions, such as palace admission, museum special exhibits, or theme parks, are generally affordable individually (roughly 1,000–5,000 KRW, about $1–4, for most palace and museum entries), but they add up if you visit several in one day. Budgeting around 10,000–20,000 KRW (about $7–15) a day for a mix of paid and free sightseeing is realistic for most itineraries.

What Other Costs Should You Plan For?

Beyond the day-to-day essentials, a few one-time or easy-to-forget costs are worth budgeting for before you land. If you haven’t already sorted your entry requirements, factor in the small K-ETA or visa fee — see our Korea entry guide to visas and K-ETA for current costs and eligibility. A local SIM card or eSIM for data typically runs a flat fee for your trip length — see our SIM card, eSIM, and WiFi egg guide to pick the right option — travel insurance is a small but worthwhile add-on, and tipping is not customary in Korea, so you can leave that line item out entirely. If you plan to shop for souvenirs, cosmetics, or electronics, remember that anything over the duty-free allowance when you fly home may need to be declared — our immigration and customs guide covers the current limits. Together, these extras usually add somewhere between 20,000–50,000 KRW (about $15–37) a day, more if shopping is a big part of your trip.

✅ Tipping is not expected in Korea.
Unlike some countries, tipping isn’t part of local dining or service culture — you can leave this out of your daily budget entirely.

How Can You Save Money on a Trip to Korea?

The fastest way to cut your Korea travel budget is to shift a few daily habits rather than skip experiences altogether. Eating one meal a day from a convenience store or traditional market instead of a restaurant, booking accommodation on weekdays instead of weekends, and relying on subway and bus with a T-money card instead of taxis can each meaningfully lower your daily spend without much sacrifice. Traveling outside the July–August and December–January peak seasons also brings both flights and accommodation prices down. None of these tips require giving up the parts of a trip that matter most — they just redirect spending toward what you actually came for.

  • Eat at least one meal a day from a convenience store, food stall, or traditional market instead of a restaurant
  • Book accommodation for weekday nights when possible — rates are often noticeably lower than weekends
  • Use a T-money (or similar) transit card for subway and bus instead of paying cash or relying on taxis
  • Prioritize free attractions — national museums, HiKR Ground, Han River parks, hiking trails, and beaches
  • Travel outside peak season (avoid July–August and December–January) for lower flight and hotel prices
  • Use free Wi-Fi at subway stations, cafes, and accommodations, and pair it with a low-cost SIM or eSIM instead of an expensive roaming plan
  • If traveling between cities, compare a KORAIL Pass against individual tickets before booking

Putting the categories above together gives a clearer picture of what a full day actually costs at each travel style. FAQ: Korea Travel Budget Questions Answered

How much does a trip to Korea cost per day on average?

Most travelers spend somewhere between 50,000 KRW (backpacker) and 250,000+ KRW (comfortable) per day, not including flights. A realistic mid-range daily budget for a first-time visitor is around 120,000–180,000 KRW (about $90–135), covering accommodation, food, local transport, and some paid attractions.

Is Korea an expensive country to visit?

Korea sits in the middle compared to other popular Asian destinations — pricier than Southeast Asia, but generally cheaper than Japan or most of Western Europe for accommodation and food. Public transportation and street food in particular are very affordable relative to the experience.

How much cash should I carry each day in Korea?

Korea is heavily card- and mobile-payment friendly, so you don’t need to carry large amounts of cash. A small amount (around 20,000–30,000 KRW) for markets, small vendors, or backup is usually enough for most days — see our currency exchange and payment methods guide for exchange, ATM, and prepaid card options.

What’s the cheapest way to eat well in Korea?

Traditional markets, street food stalls, and casual local restaurants offer the best value — a full meal often costs 8,000–15,000 KRW and comes with several free side dishes (banchan). Convenience stores are also a reliable, cheap backup for any meal.

Are there free or discounted attractions in Korea?

Yes. National museums like the National Museum of Korea and the Gyeongju National Museum are free, as are most natural attractions like Han River parks, hiking trails, and beaches. Free cultural experience spaces like HiKR Ground are also worth checking.

Is public transportation in Korea affordable?

Yes — subway and bus fares typically start around 1,400–1,600 KRW per ride, and using a rechargeable transit card gives you discounted transfers between subway and bus. Most travelers spend only 5,000–10,000 KRW a day getting around a single city.

How much should I budget for a 7-day trip to Korea?

Using the mid-range daily estimate of about 120,000–180,000 KRW, a 7-day trip works out to roughly 840,000–1,260,000 KRW (about $625–935) per person before flights. Budget travelers can expect closer to 350,000–490,000 KRW (about $260–360) for the same length of trip.

What’s the single best way to save money on a Korea trip?

Accommodation has the biggest swing between travel styles, so choosing a well-reviewed guesthouse or budget hotel over a full-service hotel, and booking weekday nights when possible, typically saves the most compared to trimming food or transport costs.
