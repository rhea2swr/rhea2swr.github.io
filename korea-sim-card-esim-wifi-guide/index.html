Landing in Korea with zero data can turn a smooth trip into a stressful one.
SIM card, eSIM, WiFi egg, or just roaming on your home plan — which one actually fits how you travel?
This guide breaks down each option so you can get connected before you even leave the airport.

Do You Need a SIM Card, eSIM, or WiFi Egg for Korea?

Most travelers to Korea need local data the moment they land, and the three realistic options are a prepaid SIM card, an eSIM, or a WiFi egg (pocket WiFi router) — roaming on your home carrier’s plan is also possible but usually the most expensive route. A prepaid SIM card is the simplest pick if you’re traveling solo and don’t mind swapping out your home SIM: it gives you unlimited data for a set number of days, commonly anywhere from 1 to 90, and can be picked up right after you land. An eSIM skips the physical card entirely — you install a data plan by scanning a QR code, often before you even board your flight — which suits travelers whose phones support it and who want to keep their home number active for calls and texts. A WiFi egg is the better fit for groups or families sharing one connection, since everyone connects to a single portable router instead of buying multiple SIMs.

In short: traveling alone with an old or dual-SIM phone → SIM card. Traveling with a modern eSIM-compatible phone and want zero physical swapping → eSIM. Traveling with 2 or more people who want to share one connection, or carrying a laptop/tablet that needs WiFi → WiFi egg. Want to keep using your home number with no setup at all and don’t mind paying more → carrier roaming.

📌 Where can I sort all of this out in one place?
Korea’s major airports — Incheon, Gimpo, Gimhae, and Jeju — all have telecom roaming centers run by the local carriers where you can register for roaming, buy a SIM card, or rent a WiFi egg or rental smartphone in one stop.

Prepaid SIM Cards in Korea: How and Where to Buy One

A prepaid SIM card is a physical card you swap into your phone that gives you a Korean number and unlimited data for a fixed period, and it’s generally cheaper than paying for roaming through your home carrier. Plans are typically sold by the day, running anywhere from a single day up to about 90 days of unlimited data, so you can match the card to the length of your trip instead of overpaying for data you won’t use. You can buy one after you land — convenience stores like CU inside Incheon International Airport and 7-Eleven locations in Seoul, Busan, and Jeju carry prepaid SIM kits — or you can pre-order online through a site like krsim.net before you fly, so it’s ready to pick up (or shipped to your hotel) as soon as you arrive.

Steps to Buy a Prepaid SIM Card

  1. Decide how many days you need data for (match it to your trip length, not longer)
  2. Choose a pickup point: airport convenience store counter, downtown 7-Eleven, or a pre-order site like krsim.net
  3. Bring your passport — Korean telecom vendors generally register SIM purchases against a photo ID
  4. Have the staff (or the pre-order instructions) swap the SIM into your phone and confirm the data connection works
  5. Keep your original home SIM somewhere safe — you’ll need it again once you’re back home
  • Best for: solo travelers who don’t mind losing access to their home number for the trip
  • Works with: any unlocked phone that takes a physical SIM tray
  • Watch out for: your home number won’t receive calls or texts while the Korean SIM is inserted, unless your phone supports a second SIM slot

eSIM for Korea: How It Works and Who It’s Best For

An eSIM is a digital SIM profile that gets installed directly onto your phone by scanning a QR code, so there’s no physical card to insert, swap, or lose. Because it’s digital, you can typically buy and install it before you even leave home — sites like esimkorea.net sell Korea eSIM plans and deliver the QR code by email, so the data plan is ready to activate the moment you land, or even earlier if you want to test the connection. The biggest advantage over a physical SIM is that many phones support running an eSIM alongside your existing home SIM at the same time, meaning you can keep receiving calls and texts on your regular number while browsing on Korean data. The catch is that not every phone supports eSIM, so it’s worth checking your device settings before you buy.

✅ How do I check if my phone supports eSIM?
Check your phone’s settings menu (usually under Settings → Cellular/Mobile → Add eSIM or similar) before you buy — most phones released in the last few years support it, but it’s worth confirming rather than assuming.

  • Best for: travelers with an eSIM-compatible phone who want to keep their home number active
  • Works with: eSIM-compatible unlocked phones only
  • Watch out for: installation needs a WiFi connection, so set it up before you leave home rather than at the airport

WiFi Egg (Pocket WiFi) Rental: How It Works and Who It’s Best For

A WiFi egg is a small portable router that creates its own WiFi hotspot, letting several phones, tablets, and laptops share one internet connection instead of each device needing its own SIM or eSIM. You rent one at the airport roaming counters — the same ones run by KT, SK Telecom, and LG U+ at Incheon, Gimpo, Gimhae, and Jeju — pick it up when you land, keep it in a bag or pocket while you’re out, and return it at the airport counter before you fly home. It’s a strong choice for groups and families since one rental covers everyone at once, and it also works well for solo travelers who need to connect a laptop or tablet in addition to a phone. The main things to plan around are keeping it charged throughout the day and returning it before departure, since WiFi eggs are rentals rather than something you keep.

⚠️ Keep it charged — and check your battery/power bank rules before you fly.
A WiFi egg runs on its own internal battery, so plan to recharge it (or carry a power bank) if you’re out all day. If you’re bringing a spare battery or power bank for it, check our guide to flying with spare batteries and power banks to Korea first — airline carry-on rules apply.

  • Best for: groups, families, or anyone connecting more than one device at once
  • Works with: any WiFi-enabled device — phones, tablets, laptops, cameras
  • Watch out for: it’s data only (no calls/texts on it), and you’re responsible for returning it before your flight

Carrier Roaming: When SKT, KT, or LG U+ Makes Sense

Carrier roaming means keeping your home SIM in your phone exactly as it is and letting your home carrier connect you to a Korean network — usually SKT, KT, or LG U+ — for the duration of your trip. It’s the option with the least setup, since there’s nothing to swap, scan, or pick up: for many carriers it activates automatically the moment you land, or with a quick roaming plan added through your carrier’s app before departure. The tradeoff is cost — roaming plans are typically pricier than a local prepaid SIM or eSIM offering the same amount of data, which is exactly why VisitKorea notes that prepaid SIM cards usually cost less than roaming services while still offering unlimited data. Roaming tends to make the most sense for short trips of just a day or two, or for travelers who simply don’t want to deal with switching SIMs at all.

If you do want to roam through a Korean carrier directly rather than your home provider, all three major carriers run roaming programs with English-language information: KT (roaming.kt.com), SK Telecom (www.sktroaming.com), and LG U+ (www.lguplus.com). Registration desks for all three are available at the airport roaming centers mentioned earlier.

SIM vs eSIM vs WiFi Egg: Which Should You Choose?

The right choice comes down to how many people and devices need to connect, and whether keeping your home number active matters to you. Solo travelers with an older or non-eSIM phone tend to do best with a prepaid SIM card, since it’s cheap and widely available at the airport the moment they land. Travelers with a modern eSIM-compatible phone who want their home number to keep working usually prefer an eSIM, since it installs before departure with no physical swap. Groups, families, and anyone juggling a laptop or tablet alongside a phone are usually better off with a WiFi egg, since one rental covers every device at once. And travelers on a very short trip who’d rather pay a bit more than deal with any setup at all can simply roam on their home carrier through SKT, KT, or LG U+.

Also Free: Public WiFi Around Korea

Even with a SIM, eSIM, or WiFi egg, it’s useful to know that most public places in Korea — airports, subway stations, train stations, and bus stops — offer free public WiFi, and 24-hour PC bangs (internet cafes) are common if you ever need a full computer. It won’t replace mobile data on the go, but it’s a handy backup if your battery is low or you’re waiting to pick up a rental.

FAQ: SIM Card, eSIM & WiFi Questions Answered

Should I get a SIM card, eSIM, or WiFi egg for my trip to Korea?

It depends on your phone and how many people need to connect. Solo travelers with a non-eSIM phone usually do best with a prepaid SIM card; travelers with an eSIM-compatible phone who want to keep their home number active usually prefer an eSIM; and groups or families sharing one connection are usually better off with a WiFi egg.

Can I buy a Korean SIM card before I land?

Yes — sites like krsim.net let you pre-order a prepaid SIM online before your flight, so it’s ready for pickup shortly after you arrive instead of waiting in line at the airport counter.

Will a WiFi egg work for multiple people at once?

Yes, that’s its main advantage over a SIM or eSIM — a single WiFi egg creates one shared hotspot that multiple phones, tablets, and laptops can connect to at the same time, which is usually cheaper than buying a SIM for every person in a group.

Do I need my passport to buy a SIM card or eSIM in Korea?

Generally yes — Korean telecom vendors typically register SIM and eSIM purchases against a photo ID, so bring your passport when you pick up or activate one, and double-check the specific requirements of whichever vendor or carrier you’re using.

What happens if my phone isn’t eSIM-compatible?

You’ll want to go with a prepaid SIM card or a WiFi egg instead. Check your phone’s settings before you fly (usually under Settings → Cellular/Mobile) to confirm whether eSIM is even an option for your device.

Is free WiFi available in Korea if I don’t want to pay for data at all?

Free public WiFi is available in most airports, subway stations, train stations, and bus stops, plus 24-hour PC bangs (internet cafes) if you need a full computer. It’s a useful backup, but it won’t give you continuous coverage while you’re out exploring, so most travelers still pair it with a SIM, eSIM, or WiFi egg.

Can I just use my home carrier’s roaming instead of buying a Korean SIM?

Yes — roaming through your home carrier, or through a Korean carrier like SKT, KT, or LG U+, requires the least setup since your existing SIM stays in place. It’s typically more expensive than a local prepaid SIM or eSIM for the same amount of data, so it tends to suit short trips more than longer stays.

Where do I pick up or return a WiFi egg rental?

At the telecom roaming counters run by KT, SK Telecom, and LG U+ inside Incheon, Gimpo, Gimhae, and Jeju airports — you pick it up when you land and return it at the same type of counter before you fly home.

Still not sure which option fits your trip? Contact the Foreigners’ General Information Center at 1345 (Weekdays 09:00–22:00) for general travel questions, or reach out directly to KT, SK Telecom, or LG U+ for carrier-specific help.
