Entering South Korea: Do I Need a Visa?

Foreign nationals entering Korea must hold a valid passport along with a Korean visa or an electronic visa obtained through the Visa Portal. However, visa-free entry is often permitted under certain conditions.

You can enter Korea without a visa in the following cases:

  • Citizens of countries or regions that have signed a visa waiver agreement with the Republic of Korea
  • Those who have received separate entry permission as stipulated by Presidential Decree
  • Those re-entering Korea with a valid re-entry permit

✅ How can I check if my nationality qualifies for visa-free entry?
Check visa-free eligibility on the official K-ETA website → “K-ETA Guide” → “Eligible Countries / Applicants.”

Visa Guide

A visa is an entry permit issued by the jurisdiction you are visiting. All foreign nationals not covered by a visa waiver agreement must hold a valid visa when entering Korea.

Types of Visas

  • Single-entry Visa: Allows entry only once before the expiration date
  • Double-entry Visa: Allows two entries before the expiration date (6 months)
  • Multiple-entry Visa: Allows multiple entries (1 year, 3 years, or 5 years)

For Tourism Purposes: Short-term General Visa (C-3)

  • Duration of Stay: Up to 90 days
  • Extension: Generally not permitted
  • Where to Apply: Korean Embassy or Consulate — in-person visit required

C-3 Visa Required Documents

Required documents may vary depending on the country of application and the embassy. Be sure to check the official guidelines of the relevant embassy.

  • Valid passport (at least 6 months remaining validity)
  • Passport-sized photo
  • Visa application form
  • Proof of financial means (e.g., bank statement)
  • Return ticket or ticket to a third country
  • Accommodation reservation confirmation
📌 Check the latest visa information:
Korea Visa Portal (www.visa.go.kr)
HiKorea (www.hikorea.go.kr)

K-ETA Guide

If you hold a passport exempt from visa requirements, you must obtain a K-ETA before boarding a flight or ship.
Valid for 3 years or until your passport expires, with unlimited entries within the validity period.

⚠️ K-ETA approval does not guarantee entry.
Final entry authorization is determined by the immigration officer at the airport.

Temporary K-ETA Exemption (until December 31, 2026)

To promote tourism in Korea, K-ETA is temporarily exempted for citizens of 22 designated countries and regions until December 31, 2026.

  • Asia: Japan, Thailand, Hong Kong, Singapore, Macau
  • Americas: United States (including Guam), Canada
  • Europe: United Kingdom, Germany, France, Italy, Netherlands, Spain, Poland, Sweden, Finland, Norway, Belgium, Denmark, Austria
  • Oceania: Australia, New Zealand

📌 Official K-ETA Website:
www.k-eta.go.kr (Korean, English, Japanese, Chinese, French, Spanish, Malay, Thai)

How to Apply for K-ETA

  1. Visit www.k-eta.go.kr → Click “Apply for K-ETA”
  2. Enter passport information
  3. Upload a facial photo (white background, no hats or sunglasses)
  4. Enter purpose of visit and intended place of stay
  5. Pay the fee (10,000 KRW, approx. $7–$8)
  6. Receive result via email (usually within 72 hours)

Requirements for K-ETA Application

  • Valid passport (at least 6 months remaining validity)
  • Valid email address
  • Facial photo (white background; no hats or sunglasses)
  • Credit or debit card

Cases Where K-ETA Is Not Required

  • Holders of diplomatic or service passports
  • Active-duty U.S. military personnel stationed in Korea (SOFA)
  • Airline crew members and seafarers
  • Transit passengers (provided they do not leave the airport)
  • Persons aged 17 or younger, or 65 or older (as of the date of entry)

⚠️ Beware of Phishing Sites

⚠️ Use only the official website.
There are many fraudulent sites impersonating K-ETA.
Official website: www.k-eta.go.kr
Official fee: 10,000 KRW (approx. $7–$8) — any site charging more is a scam.

Before You Arrive: e-Arrival Card & Final Checklist

If you don’t hold a K-ETA, you’ll also need to submit Korea’s e-Arrival Card online before you land — a separate, free document. K-ETA holders are exempt from this step. For the full e-Arrival Card walkthrough, plus immigration procedures and customs declaration limits, see our Immigration and Customs guide.

Use this checklist to confirm everything is in order before departure:

  • Confirm that your passport is valid for at least 6 months
  • Prepare your return ticket or ticket to a third country
  • Complete your K-ETA application (if applicable) or submit your e-Arrival Card
  • Register for Q-Code if departing from a designated quarantine area (recommended)
  • Prepare to declare any items exceeding the duty-free allowance

FAQ: Visa & K-ETA Questions Answered

Do I need a visa or K-ETA to enter Korea?

It depends on your nationality. If your country has a visa waiver agreement with Korea, you can enter with a K-ETA — or, until December 31, 2026, with no K-ETA at all if you’re from one of the 22 temporarily exempt countries. Otherwise, you’ll need to apply for a visa in advance through the Korea Visa Portal or a Korean embassy.

What’s the difference between K-ETA and a Korean visa?

A visa is issued by an embassy or consulate and generally requires an in-person visit and supporting documents. K-ETA is a lighter online authorization for citizens of visa-waiver countries — applied for entirely online, costing about $7–$8, and valid for 3 years or until your passport expires.

Which countries are exempt from K-ETA, and until when?

22 countries and regions are temporarily exempt from K-ETA through December 31, 2026, including Japan, the US, Canada, the UK, and most of Western Europe. Rules can change, so it’s worth checking the official K-ETA website closer to your travel date.

How long does K-ETA approval take?

Most applications are processed and emailed back within 72 hours.

Is K-ETA the same as the e-Arrival Card?

No. K-ETA is a pre-entry authorization you apply for before boarding, while the e-Arrival Card is an entry declaration required only if you don’t hold a valid K-ETA. See our Immigration and Customs guide for a full comparison and how to submit each.

How much does K-ETA cost, and how do I avoid scam sites?

The official K-ETA fee is 10,000 KRW (about $7–$8), paid only through www.k-eta.go.kr. Numerous phishing sites impersonate K-ETA and charge more — always go directly to the official government site rather than a link from an email or ad.

Can I still enter Korea without a K-ETA if my country is on the exempt list?

Yes — citizens of the 22 exempt countries don’t need K-ETA or a visa through December 31, 2026. You’ll still need to submit the e-Arrival Card before arrival, unless you choose to apply for K-ETA voluntarily.

Still have questions? Contact the Foreigners’ General Information Center at 1345 (Weekdays 09:00–22:00).
